Rohan Singh Wilkho: AI for managed water systems, Cornell University

About Me

I am an Eric and Wendy Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Civil and Environmental Engineering at Cornell University, working with Prof. Stefano Galelli. I use artificial intelligence and machine learning to study managed water systems (dams, canals, and diversions) that, along with hydroclimatic forcing, shape water flow in our rivers.

Before joining Cornell, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher and Visiting Lecturer in Civil Engineering at Texas A&M University, where I earned a PhD in Civil Engineering and a Master of Computer Science, both in 2024, advised by Prof. Nasir Gharaibeh. Earlier, I worked as a tunnel engineer on India’s first underwater tunnel project (2016–2018), after earning a bachelor’s in Civil Engineering from Jadavpur University in 2016.

Research Program

Most of the world’s major river basins are managed systems, operated by human judgment that hydrologic models cannot see and operating manuals do not record. My research program makes AI and water systems engineering inform each other: the coupling between human decisions and physical outcomes exposes limitations in predictive modeling, which cannot capture causality and human intent. These limitations motivate methodological innovations in inverse reinforcement learning, physics-informed AI, and multi-modal AI, which provide the foundation to characterize continuously evolving human-water interactions. The central goal of my research is to understand how the decision logic of managed water system operators must evolve to sustain water security and flood control under hydroclimatic non-stationarity and a changing climate.

My research program is built on three thrusts:

  1. Decoding the behavioral logic of water system operators, to recover the objectives that actually govern their decisions.
  2. Reconstructing community-scale performance records, to reveal what communities experience when those decisions fail.
  3. Diagnosing performance under compound pressures, to identify how operating logic must evolve to sustain design performance.

My AI methods and expertise have also extended to other civil engineering domains, with published studies in disaster resilience (PRIME, DFFS, FlowsDT) and infrastructure condition assessment (CulvertVision, Satellite-to-Street).
